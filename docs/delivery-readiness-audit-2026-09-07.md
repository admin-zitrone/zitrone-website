## Decision

The project has a substantial professional foundation. It is not ready for unconditional production acceptance: there is a confirmed broken Spanish contact route on the live domain, an apparent CMS privilege escalation path in source, failing quality gates, and missing verification of lead handling and production operations.

The appropriate next phase is release hardening and handoff, with targeted UX and content improvements. The evidence does not justify a wholesale rebuild.

## Scope and evidence limits

Reviewed the working checkout, current production HTTP responses, public homepage content, CMS configuration, forms, SEO, analytics, design system, tests, CI and operational documentation. Ran the production build, lint, content contracts, asset consistency check, email palette check and design audit.

The checkout already contained uncommitted pantry photography and generated-file changes. They were preserved. The build ran its normal asset/token generation steps and refreshed generated metadata. No application fixes, deployments, database writes, live submissions or messages were made for this audit.

Browser discovery returned no available browser. No fresh visual, keyboard, screen-reader, mobile interaction, Lighthouse or browser E2E pass was performed. Existing screenshot baselines and test files are evidence of infrastructure, not evidence that today's release passes. Production credentials, private analytics dashboards, actual inbox receipt, backups and account ownership were not verified. Source and production findings are distinguished below.

## Priority 0 — resolve before release acceptance

### 1. Restore the Spanish contact journey

The public homepage links to `/es/contact`; that destination returns 404. Both contact locales are generated by the successful local build.

Acceptance: deploy the intended reviewed version; verify both localized contact pages return 200; follow navigation, service and footer CTAs in both languages; complete one controlled submission per lead entry point in an agreed test environment. Confirm the stored record and the receiving team's workflow. Verify the exact production release after deployment.

### 2. Restrict changes to CMS roles

[users.ts](../src/payload/collections/users.ts) permits users to update their own record, but the `role` field has no separate access restriction. No role-protection hook appears in the collection or top-level configuration. Source analysis therefore indicates a path for an authenticated editor to assign themselves administrator privileges. This was not exploited against a live account.

Acceptance: allow only administrators to assign or change roles, with an explicit first-admin bootstrap policy; demonstrate that an editor can change allowed profile fields but cannot change their role through the API. Test in an isolated environment. Payload documents [field-level access restrictions](https://payloadcms.com/docs/access-control/fields).

### 3. Finish the lead-handling operation

[contact-request/route.ts](../src/app/api/contact-request/route.ts) saves accepted leads to Payload before acknowledging success. That is a good foundation. The route and collection have no notification or CRM delivery hook. The copy promises a human reply, but the software currently depends on someone monitoring the CMS.

Acceptance: name the responsible team, response-time commitment, fallback owner and review cadence. Either connect a dependable notification/CRM workflow or explicitly adopt a monitored CMS inbox. Verify real receipt, failure visibility, retry behavior and duplicate handling. Show the visitor a useful confirmation and an agreed expectation for follow-up.

Billing is a separate workflow: [billing-request/route.ts](../src/app/api/billing-request/route.ts) returns `503 FALLBACK_REQUIRED` and a manual email alternative when its webhook is absent. There is no durable billing-request collection in this route. The existing billing smoke test expects that fallback. Production webhook configuration and successful invoice-request delivery remain unverified. Finish and test automation if it is part of the promised deliverable; otherwise document and label the manual process accurately.

### 4. Restore meaningful release gates

Email styles currently fail their consistency check. The failing pantry contracts concern 46 words versus a 45-word budget, eight FAQs versus four, and an opening sentence that exceeds its sentence budget. These are project rules, not universal SEO requirements: decide whether the content or the rule should change, then make CI green.

Acceptance: a successful CI run for the exact release, covering build, typecheck, lint, contracts, assets, email tokens and relevant functional checks. Do not simply increase the accepted debt baseline to hide regressions.

## Priority 1 — complete the professional handoff

### Messaging and keyword strategy

The homepage's headline is broad, while its metadata and service pages already contain relevant service terminology. This is not a site with “no SEO.” However, no current research-backed keyword-to-page deliverable was found in the reviewed docs. That research may exist outside this repository.

Create one row per important page: audience, buyer problem, primary search intent, query family, location if relevant, promise, evidence and next action. Candidate families to validate include “comedores corporativos,” “servicio de comedor para empresas,” “cafetería corporativa,” “snacks para oficinas,” and “catering corporativo.” These are hypotheses, not verified search-volume findings.

A clearer candidate homepage title would be “Servicios de alimentación para empresas en México | Zitrone,” subject to approved positioning and geography. Keep the brand voice, while making the service category and fit immediately understandable. Do not force the whole service catalog into every heading.

Approve quantitative claims, current coverage, capacity, pricing conditions, certifications, testimonials and logo rights. Existing copy contains tenure and certification assertions; this audit did not independently authenticate them. Strong case studies should describe the client situation, delivered scope and a substantiated result. Identify an owner and review date for facts that can change.

For Google, AEO work largely follows search fundamentals: helpful expert content, crawlability, accurate information and indexing. Google explicitly says special schema and AI text files are unnecessary for its generative search features. Other engines have their own discovery behavior; no single checklist guarantees their citations. See [Google's guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

### Correct localized SEO wiring

[sitemap.ts](../src/app/sitemap.ts) omits contact pages. Adding them improves inventory completeness, though omission alone does not prevent indexing.

The generic [localeAlternates](../src/app/lib/seo.ts) changes the locale prefix while preserving the path. Author and reviewer names have localized slugs, yet their metadata and sitemap use that helper. Their alternates can therefore point at the opposite language's slug and rely on redirects. Use the explicit translated route mapping already used for service pages. Validate final canonical, reciprocal alternate and sitemap URLs rather than merely checking that tags exist.

### Improve inquiry fit and data accuracy

[contact-form.tsx](../src/app/[locale]/contact/contact-form.tsx) requires six stages before sending. The food stage requires a menu tier, at least one extra service and a format. This makes the journey dining-oriented even for a buyer who wants only coffee or a one-off catered event. Treat conversion impact as a hypothesis until measured, but the mismatch is observable in the source.

Provide a short initial inquiry and progressively ask service-specific qualification questions. Let buyers say “none” or “not sure” where appropriate. Preserve their selected service when entering from a service page. Ask only what the team needs at that stage.

The brief builder also writes the subsidy percentage whenever its state has a value. It initializes to 50%, so it can serialize that percentage even when the buyer did not select a subsidy. Make dependent values conditional before relying on the brief for a quote.

Contact errors lack the explicit message association used by billing. Step changes scroll the form but do not explicitly focus the new heading/control. Verify and repair the keyboard and screen-reader experience, not just the visual progress indicator.

### Wire a measurable conversion funnel

Contact view and submit-attempt events are declared in [events.ts](../src/lib/observability/events.ts), but no emitters for those events were found in the form/submission path. Accepted contact requests are tracked through server-side Vercel analytics; the shared contact submitter does not emit a browser success event to the data layer. GTM may have external rules, but they were not inspected and cannot be assumed to fill the gap.

Acceptance: verify page/CTA → form start → stage progress → validation failure → accepted lead, plus source and locale. Count success after durable acceptance. Confirm events in the actual analytics property without sending names, messages or email addresses as analytics payloads. Track qualified leads and response time, not just clicks. Align retained attribution with the client's approved privacy choices.

### Make accessibility and visual QA cover the conversion routes

The axe route list in [accessibility.spec.ts](../tests/e2e/accessibility.spec.ts) does not include `/contact`. Only the homepage appears in both languages; other templates are sampled in one language. The existing tests are useful but narrower than the documentation's broad description. The browser smoke suite does not test successful contact storage or privilege boundaries. Its billing test assumes fallback.

Visual baselines exist, but [playwright.visual.config.ts](../playwright.visual.config.ts) explains why they are excluded from the Linux CI run. Establish a reproducible visual gate and fresh approvals for mobile and desktop, both locales, menu, consent, form errors and success states. Check typography, spacing, crop quality, overflow, CTA hierarchy, motion, touch controls and translation wrapping. No present-tense visual quality score is assigned by this audit.

### Measure performance before grading it

The homepage HEAD response reported 425,774 bytes of HTML. That is a useful payload warning, not a compressed transfer measurement or a speed score. Inspect HTML/client payload, below-fold media loading, rotators, fonts and third-party scripts against a measured budget.

Target good Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: LCP ≤2.5 seconds, INP ≤200 milliseconds and CLS ≤0.1, measured separately for mobile and desktop. Use lab measurements at launch and field data when sufficient traffic exists; lab results cannot certify real-user INP. See [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).

The local image policy assumes paths are immutable for a year, but existing pantry changes replace files at the same paths. Align asset versioning with that cache policy to avoid visitors retaining older photography. This is a source/worktree consistency risk, not a demonstrated stale image in a browser.

### Tighten security and data handling

[rate-limit.ts](../src/lib/rate-limit.ts) uses an in-memory map. Its counters are local to an instance and do not provide a shared limit across serverless instances or restarts. Use a deployment-level or shared-store control appropriate to expected abuse.

[contact-requests.ts](../src/payload/collections/contact-requests.ts) describes immutable submission fields, but uses `admin.readOnly` while allowing every authenticated user to update the collection. Enforce immutable fields and lead access on the server; an admin UI setting is not an API authorization boundary.

Verify dependency advisories, deployment firewall controls, account recovery, separate environment secrets, backup coverage and approved retention/deletion procedures. This is not a penetration test or legal compliance certification. Confirm policy and actual tracker/storage behavior with the responsible client owner.

### Deliver an honest CMS and operations model

The CMS exists, but editability is partial. Service detail content is composed from TypeScript; the CMS services collection is explicitly labeled as homepage service cards. The profiler labels intentionally ignore CMS overrides. The README says production content comes from Payload while instructing maintainers to sync homepage content from code. Without an agreed policy, a later release can overwrite editorial changes.

Acceptance: document exactly what clients can edit, publish, translate and restore. Choose a clear ownership policy for each surface, demonstrate a harmless edit end to end, and test invalidation of affected pages. Alert on unexpected fallback to static content.

Refresh the runbooks: current environment/security docs still reference Sanity and old “On It” paths. The launch environment validator currently checks the public URL but does not establish that the database, secret, storage, billing and recovery workflows are operational. A passing environment command is not a handoff certificate.

Provide client access to the repository, domain/DNS, hosting, database, media storage, email service, monitoring, analytics and Search Console as applicable; record costs and renewals. Demonstrate backup restoration and deployment rollback, assign alert recipients, provide editor training and specify support/update responsibilities. These are unverified operational requirements, not assertions that the client lacks the accounts today.

## Priority 2 — controlled refactoring and growth

The system has 1,152 generated tokens and reusable design controls. Its audit currently tolerates 823 primitive leaks, 3 raw-measure issues, 13 focus-rule bypasses, 125 typography-wiring issues and 14 column-template issues. These sum to 978 code-pattern violations, not 978 independently observed visual defects.

`marketing.css` is 15,540 lines and the homepage profiler is 945 lines. File size alone is not a defect, but the combination with known token debt supports targeted cleanup. Split coherent component styles, normalize public component APIs, share form/brief behavior and reduce the baseline incrementally with visual checks. Demonstrate that adding one service or changing a type/color role does not require unrelated page patches.

After launch-critical fixes, prioritize conversion experiments, evidence-rich case studies, content maintenance and search growth using actual traffic and lead quality. Do not promise rankings, a conversion rate or AI recommendations before there is evidence.

## Practical acceptance sequence

1. Resolve the Spanish contact route and CMS role boundary.
2. Prove lead and billing delivery, responsibility and recovery.
3. Restore CI and test the actual conversion journeys, permissions and error states.
4. Approve messaging, claims, localized SEO, privacy behavior and visual/accessibility results.
5. Record production performance and analytics baselines.
6. Complete account transfer, editing documentation, backup/restore, rollback and training.
7. Ship the agreed release with a separately owned backlog for nonblocking refactoring and growth.

Professional completion means a client can trust the website, receive and act on inquiries, make the agreed edits, measure outcomes and recover from failures. The deliverable should include the evidence and operating instructions that make those capabilities dependable.
